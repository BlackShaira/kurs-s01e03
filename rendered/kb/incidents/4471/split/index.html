How the desk closed it

#4471 was closed on 5 October, with one resolution per cause:

  • parser, 379 reports — fixed, in fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. Ticket #1 in this repo is closed as done.
  • hint, 33 reports — won't fix: booking-v3 was retired, so the cause goes out with the form it came from. Ticket #2 in this repo is closed as not planned.

The warning this page carried held. The parser fix is exactly read each tenant's own order, which is what would have moved the hint reports from wrong to differently wrong. The desk settled that by retiring the form rather than by making the hint and the reader agree, so the conflict the two causes had with each other went away with booking-v3.

Why the hint cause survives the parser fix

booking-v2 prints DD/MM/YYYY under its date field, and booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY. The three tenants on booking-v3 all write the day first. Their people type the month first, because the hint tells them to, and the form stores it the tenant's way. Every report in this cause came through the form and through no other channel.

So these dates are wrong in the opposite direction from the rest of the ticket. Teach the parser to read each tenant's order and this group gets read the tenant's way too, which is not the way it was typed. The hint and the reader have to agree first: either the hint matches how the form reads, or the form reads what the hint promised.

The one-day reports, counted inside parser

Five reports describe a date one day out, not a swap. A swap turns 7 April into 4 July: the two numbers trade places. These have no second number to trade:

  • R-41202 — the invite says Tuesday 12 May, the calendar says Monday 11 May. 12 and 11 are not each other's transpose.
  • R-41620 — Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday.
  • R-41344 — the client in Lisbon got Tuesday, the client in Toronto got Monday, for one meeting.
  • R-41209 — every booking in June is one day off, and only June.
  • R-41236 — the invite arrived before the booking was made.

They are not a third cause, and they get no ticket of their own, because the kb does not file them as one. causeOf reads the tenant and the channel and never the words, so it files all five under parser. The desk had already read them differently and kept the list in desk-notes.json, and readAs counts them where they are filed and footnotes them there. That is what the table above prints. Two timezones disagreeing about which day it is, and one month behaving differently from the others, point at an offset. Whoever fixes the parser will still see these five.
